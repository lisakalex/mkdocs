Setting one up is straightforward — it's a real (if low-margin, high-volume) online business model. Here's the basic playbook:

**1. Pick a content niche**
Evergreen, high-search-volume topics: recipes, home/DIY, personal finance basics, health tips, celebrity news, "life hacks." Anything with strong search demand and low need for original reporting.

**2. Set up the site**
- WordPress is standard — cheap hosting, fast to launch
- SEO plugin (Yoast/RankMath) tuned for keyword targeting

**3. Content production**
- Freelance writers paid per-article (often $10–50/piece) via platforms like Upwork, Contentfly, or content agencies
- Increasingly, AI-drafted content with light human editing to cut costs further — many mills now run mostly on this
- Focus on volume: publish daily or multiple times a day rather than depth

**4. Monetization**
- Display ads (Google AdSense/Ezoic/Mediavine — pay-per-pageview)
- Affiliate links (Amazon Associates, etc.)
- Apply to Apple News, Google Discover, and Facebook Instant Articles for distribution — this is exactly how sites like the sewing blog get into your feed

**5. Growth loop**
- SEO-optimize headlines and titles for click-through rate over accuracy (this is where the clickbait comes from)
- Track what performs, double down on formats/topics that get clicks
- Recycle/repurpose successful headline formulas across new articles

**The honest tradeoff**: this model works financially — it's why so many of these sites exist — but it runs on volume and ad impressions rather than reader trust, so the business incentive is structurally opposed to quality. If you wanted to build something in this space but avoid becoming what you've been complaining about, the differentiator would be picking one narrow niche and actually being good at it (real expertise, longer dwell time, repeat readers) rather than competing purely on publish volume.
