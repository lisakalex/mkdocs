# Career Strategy Summary – TotalEnergies and Future Job Search

## 1. Change in mindset

Initially, I wanted to leave TotalEnergies by telling everyone how badly I had been treated and exposing the difference between my salary and the market.

After discussing it, I realised that this would probably make little difference to the company but could affect my own professional reputation.

I now think the better approach is:

* Do what is best for my career.
* Stay calm and professional.
* Let my success speak for itself.
* Move towards a better opportunity rather than away from resentment.

This is not about "being nice" to the company. It is about acting in my own long-term interests.

---

## 2. Why professionalism wins

Instead of saying:

> "You underpaid me."

The better answer is:

> "I found a better opportunity."

or

> "I've accepted a DevOps Engineer position that offers the career progression and technical development I've been looking for."

This:

* sounds confident,
* avoids arguments,
* protects my reputation,
* allows people to draw their own conclusions.

---

## 3. If someone asks why I'm leaving

Best answer:

> "I found a better opportunity that aligns with my career goals."

If they ask what makes it better:

> "It's a DevOps Engineer role with greater technical development and compensation that reflects the responsibilities."

Avoid criticising TotalEnergies.

---

## 4. If someone asks about salary

Don't volunteer my new salary to shock people.

If someone directly asks:

> "Can I ask what they're paying you?"

I can answer honestly.

There is no need to make salary comparisons to embarrass the company.

---

## 5. Farewell

Leave professionally.

Thank everyone.

Wish the team well.

Do not make a speech about being underpaid.

The real statement is moving to a better role.

---

## 6. What "vindictive" means

Vindictive means trying to punish or embarrass someone because they wronged you.

That approach rarely helps the person leaving.

---

## 7. My situation

The discussion concluded that:

* My performance is not the problem.
* My bonuses and manager feedback support that.
* The real issue appears to be role classification and career progression.

Possible explanation:

I entered the wrong salary/grade track and remained there while continuing to gain DevOps responsibilities.

---

## 8. Internal discussion

It was suggested that a career discussion with HR (James) could be useful.

Questions to ask:

* What is the difference between DevOps Technician and DevOps Engineer?
* What distinguishes C2 from NP10?
* What would be required to move to NP10?

Focus on role alignment rather than simply asking for more money.

## 10. Interviews

Around 30 first-stage interviews resulted in only one second-stage interview.

Possible reasons discussed:

* Not selling achievements strongly enough.
* Talking too much about company history instead of technical impact.
* Underselling experience.
* Limited cloud experience.
* Interview technique.

Plan:

Upload interview recordings for detailed analysis.

---

## 11. Important personal realisation

The biggest change in my thinking was this:

Originally:

> "I want everyone to know how unfairly I was treated."

Now:

> "I'm going to do what is best for my career."

I realised that:

* giving people "a piece of my mind" changes nothing,
* finding a better job changes everything.

---

## 12. Long-term philosophy

The goal is no longer to win the last day at TotalEnergies.

The goal is to win the next ten years of my career.

If I leave professionally for a better role, better salary and better future, that is a far stronger outcome than trying to embarrass the company.

---

## 13. Practical next steps

* Continue applying for DevOps Engineer positions.
* Keep improving interview performance.
* Analyse interview recordings to identify recurring issues.
* Continue internal discussions professionally while external opportunities are pursued.
* If an attractive offer arrives, resign professionally and avoid burning bridges.

---

## Final conclusion

The most important insight from this discussion was:

> **Professionalism is not something I owe the company—it is something I do for myself.**

Leaving calmly, with a better opportunity, a stronger salary, and a good reputation is the strongest outcome because it advances my career instead of focusing on the past.
